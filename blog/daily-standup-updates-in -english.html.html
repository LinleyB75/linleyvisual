Daily Stand-ups in English: How to Report Work in Progress

Published 13 November 2026 • By Linley Bignoux

stand-upsenglish-for-developersbusiness-englishspeakingphrases

The situation

Most stand-up advice assumes your work is either done or not started. Real work is usually somewhere in the middle. When I worked as a developer, I took one small feature, an automatic notification for an online school, through eight stages: checking what I was given, fixing the source, deciding what data it needed, building it, testing that data, sending it safely, testing it in layers and handing it over to the client's developer.

Every morning the question was the same: "Where are you with it?" The work was the easy part. Explaining in a few seconds which stage it had reached, what was done and what might go wrong was harder, and it's even harder in a second language.

That's the skill this post is about: reporting work in progress in a stand-up, clearly and briefly.

Why it matters

A stand-up is short, often 15 minutes for the whole team, so each update gets less than a minute. An unclear update has real costs:

  • Blockers stay hidden. If you don't say clearly that you're waiting for something, nobody helps, and the delay grows.
  • The team loses time. Long, detailed updates make everyone else wait, and the important point gets lost.
  • You look less senior than you are. A strong developer who can't explain progress clearly can come across as unsure, even when the work is excellent.

In my lessons with developers, the two most common problems are opposites: saying too much (the whole technical story) or saying too little ("Still working on it"). Both are easy to fix with a structure.

How to do it: a four-part update

The classic stand-up has three parts: what you did, what you'll do, and what's blocking you. For work in progress, add one more at the start: where it is.

  1. Where it is: name the stage. "The notification feature is at the testing stage."
  2. What you finished: one result, not the process. "Yesterday I finished the data checks. All 17 test cases pass."
  3. What's next: one clear next step. "Today I'm doing a test send to my own inbox."
  4. Blockers or risks: what you need, and from whom, or "no blockers". "I need the real policy link from the client before it can go live."

Four short sentences, about 20 seconds. If someone wants details, offer them after: "Happy to go through it after the stand-up."

Saying where you are in the process

Here is the same eight-stage feature, with one natural stand-up sentence for each stage.

StageWhat's happeningHow to say it in a stand-up
1. IntakeChecking what you were given"I've started on the notification. I'm checking the file I was given first, and it already has a few errors."
2. Fix the sourceCorrecting small errors"I'm fixing the wording and some broken links. Should be done this morning."
3. Define the dataDeciding the fields and rules"I've defined the three fields we need. I'll send the list to the backend team today."
4. BuildCompiling and checking"The build works now. It failed on a clean machine, and I've fixed that."
5. Check the dataRunning test cases"The data checks are done. All 17 test cases behave as expected."
6. Send, safelyTest sends"The dry run and sandbox are fine. Today I'm doing one real send to my own inbox."
7. Test in layersTesting on real devices"It's in final testing. I'm checking it on phones and in dark mode."
8. Hand overDocuments for the next developer"It's ready to hand over. I'm writing the setup notes for the backend team."

Useful phrases

Reporting progress

  • "I've finished… / I've got… working."
  • "I'm about halfway through…"
  • "It's at the testing stage now."

Reporting a problem you found

  • "I found a bug in… I've fixed it, and I've added a test for it."
  • "It took longer than expected because…"

Asking for help

  • "I'm blocked on… Could someone from… help after this?"
  • "I need a quick decision on… Can we take two minutes after the stand-up?"

Giving an estimate

  • "I expect to finish by…"
  • "If the keys arrive today, it should be ready on Thursday."

A full example: weak and strong

In the real project, a fresh copy of the code wouldn't build on a new machine. The cause was simple once I found it: Git doesn't keep empty folders, so the output folder was missing. Here are two ways to report that in a stand-up.

Weak update:

"Yesterday I had a problem with the build, it was not working, I was checking many things, the folder, the configuration, and finally I see that Git is not saving the empty folders, so I make a script, and now it's working I think, and today I will continue with the other things."

It's all true, but it's one long sentence, the most important point comes last, the tenses are mixed ("I see", "I make"), and "I think" and "other things" sound unsure.

Strong update:

"The notification is at the build stage. Yesterday I fixed a build failure on clean machines: Git doesn't keep empty folders, so I added a step that creates the output folder. Today I'm starting the data checks. No blockers."

Same facts, four sentences, under 20 seconds. Stage, result, next step, blockers.

Common mistakes

MistakeInstead ofSay
Present tense for finished work"Yesterday I fix the bug.""Yesterday I fixed the bug." / "I've fixed the bug."
Vague status"Still working on it.""I'm about halfway. The data checks are done, and testing is next."
Vague problem"I have a problem.""I'm blocked on the test keys from the platform team."
Sounding unsure"I will try to finish.""I expect to finish by Thursday."
"Eventually" (Portuguese eventualmente, French éventuellement)"Eventually I need help with the API." (meaning "maybe")"I might need help with the API." In English, "eventually" means "in the end, after some time".
"Actually" (French actuellement)"Actually I'm working on the tests." (meaning "now")"Right now I'm working on the tests." In English, "actually" means "in fact".
Saying yes when you didn't understand"Yes, OK.""Sorry, do you mean the staging build or production?"

Try it yourself

Take something you're working on now and write your stand-up update in four sentences: where it is, what you finished, what's next, and blockers. Read it out loud and time it. If it's over 30 seconds, cut the process and keep the results.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I say in a daily stand-up?

Three things: what you finished, what you are doing next, and anything blocking you. When your work is halfway through a longer process, also say which stage it has reached, so the team knows how close it is to done.

How long should a stand-up update be?

Usually under a minute. Give the result, not the whole story. If someone needs the details, offer to talk after the stand-up.

How do I say I'm blocked without sounding negative?

Name what you are waiting for and who can help, for example: I'm blocked on the test keys. Could someone from the platform team send them after this? A clear blocker with a request sounds professional, not negative.

What if I don't understand a question in a stand-up?

Ask for it again, or check your understanding: Sorry, could you say that again? or Do you mean the build on the staging server? Saying yes when you didn't understand causes more problems than asking.
