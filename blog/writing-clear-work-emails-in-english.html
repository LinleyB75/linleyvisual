Writing Short, Clear Work Emails in English

Published 13 November 2026 • By Linley Bignoux

business-englishemailswritingtonefalse-friends

The situation

When I worked as a developer, I fixed a small layout problem: images in a two-column section were shrinking and spreading apart in some webmail applications. The fix took minutes. The email to the client about it mattered more: what changed, whether they needed to do anything, and when it would be ready.

Most work emails are like that. The reader is busy, reads on a phone, and wants to know three things quickly: what this is about, what you need, and by when.

Why it matters

  • Unclear emails get no reply, or the wrong reply.
  • A specific subject line gets opened and is easy to find later.
  • Clear, polite emails build your reputation with people you may never meet.

How to do it: five parts

  1. A specific subject line.
  2. The purpose in the first sentence.
  3. The key details, short, with a list if there are several.
  4. A clear action and deadline.
  5. A short, polite close.

Subject lines

Instead ofTry
"Question""Question about the March invoice (order 4471)"
"Update""Newsletter images fixed: ready to send"
"Meeting""Can we move Thursday's meeting to 3 pm?"
"Urgent!!!""Action needed by Friday: delivery address for order 4471"

Tone: direct and polite

Too directPolite and still clear
"I want the file.""I'd like the file, if possible."
"Send me the report.""Could you send me the report?"
"ASAP.""By Thursday at 12."
"You didn't answer.""Just following up on my email from Monday."

A full example: weak and strong

Weak:

Subject: Update

Dear Sir, I write to inform you that about the problem of the images which was happening in some of the emails, I made some changes in the code and the padding and now I think it is ok, waiting your answer.

Strong:

Subject: Newsletter images fixed: ready to send

Hi Anna,

Quick update: I've fixed the images that were spreading apart in the two-column section. I've tested the newsletter in Gmail, Outlook and Apple Mail, and it looks right in all three.

Could you approve it by Thursday at 12, so it goes out on Friday as planned?

Thanks,
Daniel

The subject says what happened, the first line gives the purpose, the details are short, and the action has a deadline.

Common mistakes

MistakeInstead ofSay
Too formal and translated"I write to inform you that…""I'm writing to let you know that…"
Missing "for""Waiting your answer.""I look forward to hearing from you." / "Let me know what you think."
"Dear Sir" when you know the name"Dear Sir,""Hi Anna," / "Dear Ms Silva,"
"Assist" (Portuguese assistir, Spanish asistir)"I will assist the meeting.""I will attend the meeting." In English, "assist" means "help".
No actionThe email just ends"Could you confirm by Friday?"

Try it yourself

Open the last work email you sent. Rewrite it with a specific subject line, the purpose in the first sentence, and one clear action with a deadline. Is it shorter? It usually is.

Frequently Asked Questions

How should I start a work email?

With a greeting and then your purpose in the first sentence: Hi Anna, I'm writing about the March invoice. If you already know the person well, Quick update on the March invoice is fine.

How do I end a work email in English?

Finish with the action you need, then a short closing: Could you confirm by Friday? Thanks, Daniel. Best regards and Kind regards are safe for most work emails.

How long should a work email be?

Usually short enough to read without scrolling: a subject, three or four short paragraphs at most, and one clear action. If it's longer, consider a call or an attachment.
