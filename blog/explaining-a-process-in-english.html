Explaining a Process in English: Steps, Sequence and Who Does What

Published 13 November 2026 • By Linley Bignoux

business-englishprocesseslogisticsgrammarphrases

The situation

For 18 years I worked in stock control and inventory, and a lot of that work was explaining processes: how goods are received, checked, stored, picked and dispatched, and who signs off each step. Later, as a developer, I explained how a system worked in five layers, from the design to the customer's screen.

Different jobs, same skill. A process explanation has to be in the right order, say who is responsible, and be easy to follow the first time.

Why it matters

  • New colleagues learn the process from you. If the explanation is unclear, mistakes are built in from day one.
  • Clients and auditors judge your organisation by how clearly you describe what you do.
  • Problems often happen between steps, so it matters who does what, and when.

How to do it

  1. Give an overview: "There are five main steps. I'll go through them in order."
  2. Go through the steps with sequencing words.
  3. Say who is responsible for each step.
  4. Mention the checks: what can go wrong, and how it's caught.
  5. Summarise and ask for questions.

Sequencing words

  • "First, … Then … Next, … After that, … Finally, …"
  • "Once the order is approved, …"
  • "Before dispatching the goods, we…" / "After checking the goods, we…"

The passive voice for processes

In a process, the action usually matters more than the person, so English often uses the passive voice: is / are + past participle.

ActivePassive
"We check the goods against the order.""The goods are checked against the order."
"Someone reviews the code.""The code is reviewed."
"The system sends a confirmation.""A confirmation is sent."

Use the active voice when you want to show responsibility: "The warehouse supervisor signs off the count."

Two full examples

Receiving goods in a warehouse:

"There are five steps. First, the delivery is checked against the purchase order. Then any damaged items are recorded and photographed. Next, the goods are entered into the stock system. After that, they're put away in their locations. Finally, the supervisor signs off the delivery, and any differences are reported to purchasing."

Releasing a software change:

"There are four steps. First, the change is reviewed by another developer. Once it's approved, the automated tests are run. If they pass, the change is merged into the main code. Finally, it's deployed to production, and the team lead confirms that everything works."

Who does what

  • "The warehouse team is responsible for…"
  • "That step is handled by…"
  • "The supervisor signs off…" / "Finance approves…"

Common mistakes

MistakeInstead ofSay
"After to" + verb"After to check the goods…""After checking the goods…"
Missing past participle"The goods are check.""The goods are checked."
No overviewStarting with step one"There are five main steps."
Repeating one word"Then… then… then…"Mix them: "then, next, after that, once that's done"
Unclear responsibility"Somebody checks it.""The supervisor checks it."

Try it yourself

Choose a process from your work with four to six steps. Explain it out loud with an overview, sequencing words, at least two passive sentences, and who is responsible for each step.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I use the passive voice?

When the action matters more than the person doing it, which is usual in processes: The goods are checked against the order. Use the active voice when you want to say who is responsible: The warehouse team checks the goods.

Which words show the order of steps?

First, then, next, after that, once that's done, and finally. Before and after are followed by a noun or an -ing verb: after checking the goods, not after to check.

How do I start explaining a long process?

Give an overview first: There are five main steps. I'll go through them in order. The listener can then follow where you are.
