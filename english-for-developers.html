Who these lessons are for

Developers who read documentation and write code in English every day, but find speaking and writing under pressure harder: in a stand-up, a code review, a meeting with a client or a job interview.

A good fit

  • Developers, QA, DevOps and data engineers in international or remote teams
  • You understand meetings well, but speaking up feels slow or stressful
  • You want review comments and messages that sound clear and professional
  • You're preparing for interviews with international companies
  • Tech leads who present to managers, clients or other teams

Not a fit

  • Children and teenagers: these lessons are for adults
  • Exam-only courses, such as IELTS preparation
  • Learning to code: we practise the English, not the programming

What I help you fix

Going quiet in stand-ups and meetings

You know what you want to say, but by the time the sentence is ready the conversation has moved on. We build ready-made phrases so you can join in at the right moment.

Review comments that sound rude

Short, direct comments can read as harsh in writing. You learn to suggest, question and push back in a pull request so your point lands and nobody is offended.

Explaining tech to non-technical people

Managers and clients don't need the implementation details. We practise plain-English explanations of what you built, what broke and why it takes longer.

Demos that lose the room

Structure and signposting for demos, design walkthroughs and sprint reviews, plus practice answering the questions you didn't prepare for.

Tickets nobody understands

Bug reports, tickets and status updates that state the problem, the steps, what you expected and what happened, so nobody has to come back and ask.

Interview answers below your real level

Your skills are strong, but your answers come out short or unclear. We practise talking about your projects and decisions until your English matches your experience.

English for every stage of a sprint

A sprint, and one phrase for each stage

Each stage has its own language. In lessons we practise the full conversations, not just single phrases.

  1. 1. Sprint planningEstimating work and raising risks“I think this is closer to five points than three.”
  2. 2. Daily stand-upProgress, next steps and blockers“I'm blocked until I get access to staging.”
  3. 3. Code reviewSuggesting and questioning changes“Could we pull this into a helper function?”
  4. 4. Sprint reviewDemoing your work“Let me show you how it works from the user's side.”

Say it better at work

Small changes in wording make a big difference to how you come across. A few examples of what we practise:

Situation Instead of Try
Code review“This is wrong.”“I think this might fail when the list is empty. Could we add a check?”
Stand-up blocker“I have a problem.”“I'm blocked on the payment API. I need the test keys from the platform team.”
Estimating“It's impossible.”“That's tight. I can finish the core part by Friday, or all of it by next Wednesday.”
Explaining a delay“It's not ready.”“It's taking longer because we found a bug in the old code. I expect to finish by Thursday.”
Disagreeing“No, that's bad.”“I see the benefit, but I'm worried it'll be hard to maintain.”
Didn't understand“What?”“Sorry, could you say that again? I didn't catch the last part.”

Frequently Asked Questions

What level of English do I need?

Lessons are built for people who already use English at work, even if speaking still feels difficult. In the free trial I check your level and tell you honestly whether these lessons are the right fit.

Do you understand technical vocabulary?

Yes. I have worked as a developer myself, in HTML, CSS, JavaScript and Node.js, so I know words like pull request, merge conflict, deployment and sprint, and you don't need to explain your job before we can work on your English.

Can we use my real work in lessons?

Yes, and it works best that way. Bring stand-up notes, review comments, tickets or slides you're allowed to share, with anything confidential removed, and we practise with them.

Can you help me prepare for a technical interview in English?

Yes, the English side of it: talking about your experience and projects, explaining your technical decisions, thinking out loud while you solve a problem, and asking good questions at the end. We don't practise the coding itself.

Can you help with written English, like code review comments and tickets?

Yes. We work on review comments, pull request descriptions, bug reports, status updates and chat messages, so they are clear, polite and quick to read.

How do I book, and where do lessons take place?

Use any Book button on this page. It takes you to my booking page, where you choose a time and pay securely. Lessons are one to one and online, in a virtual classroom with video, a whiteboard and shared documents.

How much do lessons cost?

A free 20-minute trial, then $16 for a single 60-minute lesson, $15 per lesson in a pack of 5, or $14 per lesson in a pack of 10 or 20. Prices are in US dollars.

What time zone are you in?

My time zone is GMT+4. That gives good overlap with Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Asia during the day, and with South America in my afternoon and evening.
