What We Work On

Lessons are built around the situations you face at work. These are the ones my students ask for most.

Speaking • Daily

Stand-up Updates

Say what you did, what you're doing next and what's blocking you in under a minute, clearly and without over-explaining.

Writing • Code Reviews

Code Review Comments

Suggest a change, ask a question or push back on a pull request in English that is clear and polite, not blunt or vague.

Speaking • Meetings

Sprint Planning & Meetings

Estimate work, raise risks and dependencies, agree and disagree, and make sure decisions are clear before the meeting ends.

Speaking • Presenting

Presentations & Demos

Walk a team through a feature, a system design or a demo step by step, and handle the questions afterwards.

Speaking • Stakeholders

Explaining Tech to Non-Technical People

Explain what you built, what went wrong or why something will take longer, in plain English that managers and clients understand.

Writing • Tickets

Bug Reports & Written Updates

Write tickets, incident updates and status messages that say what happened, why and what's next, so nobody has to ask.

Speaking • Calls

Client & Team Calls

Small talk, clarifying questions and polite follow-ups on video calls with colleagues and clients in other countries.

Speaking • Careers

Job Interviews in English

Talk about your experience and projects, explain your technical decisions and answer common interview questions with confidence.

What I Help You Fix

Going Quiet in Meetings

You understand everything, but by the time you've built the sentence the conversation has moved on. We practise ready-made phrases so you can join in at the right moment.

Stand-ups That Run Too Long

A simple structure for updates that are short, specific and easy to follow, even when the work behind them is complicated.

Review Comments That Sound Rude

Direct English can read as harsh in writing. You'll learn how to suggest, question and disagree in code reviews without upsetting anyone or being misunderstood.

Explaining Your Work

Describing a system, a change or a trade-off so that both engineers and managers can follow it.

Presentations That Lose People

Structure, signposting and timing for demos and presentations, plus practice answering the questions you didn't prepare for.

Unclear Tickets & Bug Reports

Tickets and reports that state the problem, the steps to reproduce it, what you expected and what actually happened.

Messages That Don't Get Answered

Emails and chat messages with a clear request and a clear deadline, at the right level of formality.

Disagreeing Politely

Pushing back on an estimate, a design or a deadline in a way that is honest and professional.

Being Asked to Repeat Yourself

Pronunciation, word stress and pace, focused on the technical words you use every day.

Small Talk

The informal conversation before and after meetings that helps you build good working relationships with your team.

Interview Nerves

Practising answers about your experience and projects until you can give them clearly under pressure.

Business English Beyond Tech

English for operations, logistics, supply chain and project management, from a teacher who spent 18 years in stock control and inventory.

English for Your Working Week

Most of the English you need at work comes up in the same few situations every week. These are the ones we practise.

A sprint in a tech team, and the English it needs

Each stage of the sprint has its own language. We practise each one with your real examples.

  1. 1. Sprint planningEstimating work, raising risks, agreeing on prioritiesLesson focus: meetings
  2. 2. Daily stand-upWhat you did, what's next, what's blocking youLesson focus: stand-ups
  3. 3. Code reviewSuggesting, questioning and approving changesLesson focus: written feedback
  4. 4. DemoShowing your work and answering questionsLesson focus: presentations
  5. 5. RetrospectiveWhat went well, what didn't, what changes next timeLesson focus: discussion
Situation What often goes wrong What we practise Example phrase
Stand-up Too much detail, no clear blocker A three-part update “Yesterday I finished the login fix. Today I'm testing it. I'm blocked on access to staging.”
Code review Comments read as orders Suggesting and asking “Could we move this into a helper function? It might make it easier to test.”
Meeting Waiting too long to speak Joining in politely “Sorry to jump in, could I add something here?”
Client call Too much technical detail Plain-English explanations “In short, the page was slow because it loaded everything at once. We've fixed that.”
Presentation No clear structure Signposting “First I'll show the problem, then the fix, and then I'll take your questions.”

Every lesson is one to one and online, planned around your role and your goals. Lessons take place in Classgap's online classroom, which has video, a whiteboard and shared documents.

You book and pay through my booking page, where you can choose single lessons or packages.

How a lesson works

  • Warm-up: a short conversation about your week, which often gives us real material for the lesson.
  • Focus: one situation, such as a stand-up update or a review comment, and the language you need for it.
  • Practice: role-plays based on your real work, repeated until the language feels natural.
  • Feedback: clear corrections on vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation, so you know what to work on next.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who are your lessons for?

Adults who use English at work, especially software developers, engineers, IT support teams and project managers in international teams. I also teach Business English to professionals in other fields, including operations, logistics and management, and to university students preparing for work.

What do we practise in a lesson?

The situations you face at work: daily stand-ups, code reviews, sprint planning, presentations and demos, client calls, written updates and job interviews. We work with your real examples where you can share them, then practise until the language feels natural.

How do I book a lesson?

Use the Book a lesson button on this page. It takes you to my booking page, where you choose a time, pay securely and join the lesson online. You can start with a free 20-minute trial, then book single lessons or packs of 5, 10 or 20 lessons.

Have you actually worked in tech?

Yes. I have also worked as a freelance developer, building and testing emails and websites in HTML, CSS, JavaScript and Node.js, and my code is on GitHub. I know the tools, the meetings and the vocabulary, so we can spend the lesson on your English rather than on explaining your job.

Can you help me prepare for a job interview in English?

Yes. We practise common interview questions, talking about your experience and projects, explaining technical decisions clearly, and asking good questions at the end. For technical roles, we also practise talking through a problem out loud.

Do you teach IELTS?

I am trained to teach IELTS, with certificates that include IELTS teacher training from IDP and courses in teaching IELTS Writing and Speaking. My main focus is English for work, so if you need IELTS, tell me your target score and test date and we can talk about whether my lessons are the right fit.

I don't work in tech. Can I still book lessons?

Yes. I teach Business English for meetings, negotiations, presentations, emails and interviews. Before teaching, I spent about 18 years in stock control and inventory management, so I also understand operations, logistics and supply chain work.

What time zone are you in?

My time zone is GMT+4. That gives good overlap with Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Asia during the day, and with South America in my afternoon and evening.
