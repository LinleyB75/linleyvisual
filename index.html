Transactional Emails & Handoff

I design, develop and test transactional emails: the messages your system sends when someone signs up, buys, books or needs to confirm something, set up at your sending provider with my side of the API written and tested. I can also update the emails you already have.

Each email is designed and built in HTML/CSS or MJML, tested in major email clients, and set up in your email platform.

How the email gets sent

  • Hosted by the provider: your application sends the email's ID and a JSON object of values in one request. Your team can update the wording without changing anything in your application.
  • My side of the API: I write a small, tested Node.js reference for the send step, with a dry run, a sandbox call and a live mode. Your team deploys and runs it.

What I build

  • Emails I build: account setup and welcome emails, email verification and double opt-in, password resets and 2FA codes, order confirmations and receipts, booking and class confirmations and reminders, and account notifications.
  • Updating existing emails: fixing rendering problems, adding dynamic content, or adapting an email you already use, then re-testing it before it goes back into your platform.
  • HTML/CSS and MJML design and development: layouts I design and build to hold up across email clients, including Outlook's Word engine.
  • Dynamic content: personalisation written in Handlebars or your provider's own tag language. That includes fallbacks for missing data (for example "Hi there" when there's no first name), and conditional blocks and loops where your provider supports them.

Testing, setup and handoff

  • Testing: major clients including Outlook, Apple Mail, Gmail and dark mode, plus test sends with unusual data such as missing or very long names.
  • Provider setup: the email set up at your sending provider and found by its ID, with its tags and fallbacks configured.
  • Handoff: the email's ID, a table of the fields it needs (name, format, required or optional) and a sample JSON request.
  • Domain authentication: SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up and checked. I need access to your domain's DNS.
  • Payload checks and tests: a field table plus scripted test cases for missing, very long and special-character data, run before the email goes into your platform.

Another provider that sends by ID? Ask me. What I don't do: connect your website, shop or application to your provider. I set up the email and define the data, and your team connects it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you build emails from Figma, Sketch or Photoshop designs?

Yes. I convert designs from Figma, Adobe XD, Photoshop, Illustrator, Sketch, and image files into responsive HTML emails that match the design as closely as each email client allows. I can also design the email myself and then build it. I offer white-label services for digital agencies, as well as direct support for small businesses, individual entrepreneurs, designers, and developers.

Why hire an Email Developer over using a drag-and-drop builder?

Drag-and-drop builders definitely have their place and usage, especially for design mockups, rapid prototyping, and building simple to intermediate layouts. However, when scaling or building complex designs, they can generate bloated markup that gets clipped in Gmail, breaks in legacy Outlook versions, or scrambles in Dark Mode. Hand-built emails overcome these limits, delivering consistent rendering across major email clients and unlocking advanced dynamic data loops (Handlebars and your provider's own tag language).

Which sending providers do you work with?

I build for transactional providers that keep the email on their side and send it by its ID, so your application only passes the data: Brevo, Postmark, SendGrid and MailerSend. Dynamic content is written in Handlebars or your provider's own tag language, with a fallback for anything missing.

How does hiring an Email Developer in Mauritius benefit global clients?

Operating in GMT+4 provides practical time-zone overlap with Europe, the UK, Australia, and early US East Coast mornings. This allows for smooth daily hand-offs and fast overnight turnarounds for Western agencies and international clients.

What transactional emails do you build?

I build account setup and welcome emails, email verification and double opt-in, password resets and 2FA codes, order confirmations and receipts, booking and class confirmations and reminders, and account notifications. I can also update transactional emails you already have.

How is a transactional email sent once you've built it?

Your application sends one request to your provider's API with the email's ID and a JSON object of values. The email is stored at the provider, so your team can update the wording without changing anything in your application. I define the values and write my side of that request: a tested Node.js reference for the send step. Your team connects it and keeps the API key.

Do you write the backend that sends the emails?

I don't build or run your live system. I design, develop and test the email, set it up at your sending provider and give your team the email's ID, a table of the fields it needs and a sample JSON request. I also write my side of the API: a tested Node.js reference implementation of the sending step, so your team has a working reference to adapt. Your team deploys it, stores the security tokens and connects your website or application.

Can you update a transactional email we already have?

Yes. I can fix rendering problems, add dynamic content such as a fallback for a missing first name, or adapt an email you already use, then re-test it in major email clients before it goes back into your platform.

What do you test in a transactional email before it goes live?

I test the email with missing, very long and special-character data, check the payload against the field table, and check the delivered email in major email clients, including dark mode. No test covers every client, so tell me which ones your customers use and I'll check those first.
